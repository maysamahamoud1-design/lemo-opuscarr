# Facts and their basis

The film gives no numbers beyond month names; its claims are standard climatology of the Horn of Africa. Schematic parts are labelled "Jaantus fudud" (simplified diagram) on screen.

- **Two rainy seasons:** Gu' (about April–June) and Deyr (about October–December); dry seasons Jilaal (about January–March) and Xagaa (about July–September). Basis: FAO SWALIM climate descriptions of Somalia; Somali seasonal calendar.
- **Cause:** the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ), where the north-east and south-east trade winds converge near the equator; warm moist air rises, cools and rains. It migrates north and south with the overhead sun, crossing Somalia northward in boreal spring and southward in autumn. Basis: standard tropical climatology (e.g. WMO and NOAA educational material).
- **Shown positions are schematic:** the belt's latitude follows a smoothed monthly table (≈45°E: Jan −12°, Apr 0°, Jul 15°, Oct 4°); the real ITCZ over East Africa is broad and irregular.
- **Xagaa:** the south-west monsoon (Somali Jet) blows roughly parallel to the coast; it drives coastal upwelling of cold water, which cools the air and suppresses rain along the coast. Basis: oceanography of the Somali Current upwelling.
- **Jilaal:** the north-east monsoon brings dry air from the north-east. Basis: as above.
- **Section:** real sea-floor profile from AWS Terrain Tiles along 47.2°E 9.4°N → 53.2°E 5.2°N, ×12 vertical; the warm/cold water layers in it are schematic.
