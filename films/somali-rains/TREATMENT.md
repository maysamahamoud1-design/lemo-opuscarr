# Labada Xilli-roobaad ee Soomaaliya: treatment

Fahaan channel · Somali-language YouTube Short · 1080×1920, 24 fps, 56.1 s. Voice: Azure `so-SO-MuuseNeural` (the user's own key). No music, no pitched sound, no faces or figures. Style: 3D Map Explainer (`map-explainer`), with the Fahaan theme on top (karaoke captions in Montserrat ExtraBold, the season calendar strip, the Fahaan icon end card).

(An earlier draft of this treatment was written for Isometric Infographic, then Low-poly Island; the user chose 3D Map Explainer. The story below is unchanged; the shots are rewritten for real terrain.)

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **A list of four seasons**, one place per season. Clear, but it describes instead of explaining *why*.
2. **Two places compared** (a wet Somalia and a dry one). Strong contrast, but it splits a vertical frame and hides the one mechanism.
3. **One tracked subject over one real landscape (chosen).** The subject is the rain belt (the ITCZ), a glowing band with clouds along it, sliding north and south over the real Horn of Africa, while a calendar strip's marker follows the months. It crosses Somalia twice a year: that is the answer. Then the turn: why the months in between stay dry (monsoon along the coast and upwelling; a dry north-east wind).

## 2. Logline and arc

A belt of rain that follows the sun crosses Somalia twice a year, so Somalia has two rainy seasons.
Setup (0–11 s): the question, the two seasons on the calendar. Mechanism (11–31 s): what the ITCZ is, why it rains there, that it follows the sun. Payoff (31–38 s): the two crossings, the land greens. Turn (38–52 s): Xagaa and Jilaal stay dry; the sea is cut open to show cold water rising. End (52–56 s): the Fahaan end card.

## 3. Benchmark

Learn from news 3D terrain explainers (one glowing data layer over real ground, a cut through the ground for what lies under it) and from atlas climate plates (a calendar under the map). Take none of their colours, icons, typefaces or camera paths.
